How a phone talks to a watch

A short read · 3 min

Bluetooth Low Energy sends data in short bursts called connection events. The phone and the gadget agree on an interval, often 15 milliseconds, and wake together at each event to swap a few packets before going back to sleep.

Each packet carries at most 251 bytes, and a fast link fits several packets into one event. That is how a stream of a few hundred kilobits per second fits through a radio that spends most of its time asleep.

When nothing changes on the screen, nothing needs to be sent at all. The gadget keeps its last picture on the panel, and the radio can skip events until there is news.

When something does change, only the difference travels: a ticking second hand costs about a kilobyte, a whole new page a few dozen.
